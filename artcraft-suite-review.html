ArtCraft Suite Review: All 7 Open-Source Adobe Alternatives, Rated

Seven apps. Zero dollars. One small team, a Rust toolchain, and a very big promise: rebuild Adobe Creative Cloud as free, open-source software. We went through the whole Crafting Apps suite to separate what's real from what's vapor — and to answer the only question that matters: can any of this replace your Adobe subscription today?

The money at stake: Adobe All-Apps runs ~$59.99/mo ≈ $720/year. The ArtCraft equivalent: $0. That's the entire reason this project exists — and why it went viral.

What the suite gets right

  • A unified architecture. All seven apps share one philosophy: native Rust performance, GPU rendering (Metal/Vulkan/DX12/WebGPU), no Electron, no telemetry, no account. Consistency Adobe itself doesn't have across its 35-year-old codebase layers.
  • File-format first. The family strategy is to open the incumbent's files — PhotoCraft's layered PSD support (134/135 round-trip tests, including CMYK and 16/32-bit documents) is the proof of concept. If similar fidelity lands for Illustrator, Premiere and InDesign files, the suite becomes genuinely disruptive.
  • Ambitious where it counts. FilmCraft implements its own codecs instead of wrapping FFmpeg; LightCraft writes its own RAW decoder. These are multi-year bets on independence that no other free alternative has attempted.
  • Real openness. Source on GitHub, free for commercial use, auditable by anyone. And the sister product — the ArtCraft AI studio — suggests where AI generation meets classic editing next.

Where the suite falls short — honestly

  • Most of it doesn't exist yet. Five of seven apps are pre-alpha. Reviewing a suite that is mostly a roadmap means reviewing promises — and this project's timelines have already slipped once.
  • The parity gap is real. PhotoCraft, the most advanced app, covers roughly a quarter to a third of Photoshop. The missing portion is exactly what professionals use daily: smart objects, plugins, deep automation.
  • Small-team risk. A codebase this ambitious, built this fast, raises legitimate maintenance questions — a 2026 Sonar survey found 96% of developers don't fully trust AI-generated code. Bus factor concerns apply.
  • Legal overhang. Experts have flagged trade-dress risk in mimicking Adobe's UI pixel-for-pixel. Probably a project problem, not a user problem — but it's unresolved.

Should you cancel Adobe? (Decision guide)

  • Your Adobe usage = occasional PSD edits + PDFs → You can realistically cancel now. PhotoCraft + PrintCraft + Photopea as backup covers you, saving ~$180–276/year.
  • You're a photographer on the Photography plan → Wait for LightCraft's catalog-import story. Try darktable in the meantime — it's free and mature.
  • You're a working designer/editor with clients → No. Keep Creative Cloud; experiment with the Crafting Apps on the side and re-check each quarter.
  • You're a student or hobbyist → Start on the free tools. You may never need Adobe at all.

The bottom line

ArtCraft's Crafting Apps are simultaneously the most exciting open-source release of 2026 and a suite that is 5/7ths vapor. The rational move: use what's alpha, watch what's coming, and don't burn your Adobe bridge until the parity numbers — not the star counts — say you can. We update this review as each app ships.

Frequently asked questions

What apps are in the ArtCraft Crafting Apps suite?

Seven: PhotoCraft (Photoshop), VectorCraft (Illustrator), FilmCraft (Premiere Pro), LightCraft (Lightroom), PrintCraft/PdfCraft (Acrobat), EffectCraft (After Effects) and DesignCraft (InDesign). All free, open-source, written in Rust.

Is the ArtCraft suite ready to replace Adobe Creative Cloud?

No. Only PhotoCraft and PrintCraft have reached early alpha; five apps are still in development. Fine for hobbyists, not for professional pipelines.

Is ArtCraft legal and safe?

The code is open-source and auditable. Trade-dress questions about mimicking Adobe's UI are unresolved but unlikely to affect users. The practical risk is fake download sites — use only getartcraft.com and github.com/storytold.

How much money would switching save?

Theoretically $180–720/year depending on your Adobe plan — but as of October 2026 the suite isn't complete enough for most professionals to switch.

Who is behind ArtCraft?

Brandon Thomas, an Atlanta-based developer and former CEO of storyteller.ai, building the apps in Rust with heavy AI-assisted coding since late September 2026.
