PhotoCraft Review: The Open-Source Photoshop Alternative, Examined Honestly

Early alphaFree & open-source

PhotoCraft is the most-watched piece of free creative software of October 2026 — a from-scratch, Rust-written Photoshop alternative in ArtCraft's Crafting Apps family. But GitHub stars aren't a review. Here's an honest examination of what the alpha does well, where it breaks, and who should use it.

The verdict up front

PhotoCraft is the most impressive "week one" image editor ever shipped — and it's still a week-one app. The PSD compatibility is real, the color management is shockingly serious for a free tool, and the price (free, open-source) is unbeatable. But with roughly a quarter to a third of Photoshop's feature set and alpha-stage stability, it's a tool for hobbyists, quick edits and the curious — not yet for deadline-driven client work.

What PhotoCraft gets right

1. Real PSD support — the headline feature

PhotoCraft opens and saves layered PSD and PSB files, passing 134 of 135 round-trip tests in the project's compatibility suite. Layers, masks and blend modes largely survive the trip, and the app implements the everyday Photoshop surface: layers, masks, adjustment layers, layer styles, text, vectors and brushes. If your main need is "open a PSD, fix something, save it back" without paying Adobe, this alone justifies the download. Details in our PSD compatibility guide.

2. Serious color management — the underrated feature

This is what separates PhotoCraft from every "free Photoshop clone" before it: it handles RGB, CMYK, Lab and grayscale documents at 8, 16 and 32-bit depth, with ICC color management and soft proofing. GIMP still struggles with CMYK; PhotoCraft treats print workflows as a first-class citizen from day one. For designers who occasionally need to check a print file, that's a genuine reason to keep it installed.

3. Native performance

Written in pure Rust with GPU rendering via Metal, Vulkan, DirectX 12 and WebGPU — no Electron bloat, no background services. The app launches fast and stays responsive on large canvases.

4. Open by design

The code is public on GitHub, so security researchers can audit it — meaningful for software you'll open client files with. And because it's part of the ArtCraft family, it shares DNA with the ArtCraft AI studio: expect generation features to meet editing features sooner than Adobe users might like.

Where PhotoCraft falls short

  • Partial feature parity. Roughly a quarter to a third of Photoshop's feature set. Smart objects are the biggest miss; some advanced layer and color features are still in progress.
  • Alpha stability. Expect occasional crashes on edge-case files. Save often; keep originals untouched.
  • A moving target. The roadmap is ambitious and timelines have already slipped once. Treat promises as aspirations, not dates.
  • Unanswered questions. Legal experts have raised trade-dress concerns about a pixel-similar Photoshop UI. Unlikely to affect users directly, but it adds roadmap uncertainty — know it before building a workflow on PhotoCraft.

Frequently asked questions

Is PhotoCraft good?

For an early alpha, yes — layered PSD read/write, CMYK/ICC color management, native Rust speed, free and open-source. But at roughly a third of Photoshop's feature set, it suits hobbyists and quick edits rather than professional production.

Is PhotoCraft safe to use?

The code is open-source and auditable. The real risk is fake download sites — only use getartcraft.com or github.com/storytold. As alpha software, keep backups of important files.

What are PhotoCraft's biggest limitations?

No smart objects, incomplete advanced features, and alpha-stage instability — roughly a quarter to a third of Photoshop's feature set as of October 2026.

Does PhotoCraft support CMYK for print work?

Yes — RGB, CMYK, Lab and grayscale at 8/16/32-bit, with ICC color management and soft proofing. A standout among free editors.

Does PhotoCraft work offline?

Yes — it's a native desktop app with no account requirement and no cloud dependency.
