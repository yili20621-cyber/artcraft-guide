VectorCraft: ArtCraft's Open-Source Illustrator Alternative

In developmentFree & open-source

VectorCraft is the vector editor in ArtCraft's Crafting Apps family — a from-scratch, Rust-written reimplementation of Adobe Illustrator, free forever and open-source. Its tagline: "Vector illustration, reimagined in pure Rust." Here's what's actually implemented, what's claimed, and what to use while it matures.

Status as of October 2026

VectorCraft is in development — one stage behind PhotoCraft, the family's early-alpha flagship. Builds and source are distributed through the official channels: getartcraft.com/apps and the github.com/storytold organization. Given how fast this project ships, expect iteration to be quick — but treat all roadmap statements as aspirations, not dates.

The feature set (as published by the project)

  • Core vector editing: pen tool, direct selection, Boolean operations on shapes, and snapping — the fundamental Illustrator workflow.
  • Advanced shape tools: gradient meshes, blending, and envelope transformations — features many free vector editors never implement.
  • Image tracing: converts raster images into vectors, the single most-used "pro" feature for logo cleanup work.
  • File formats: SVG and PDF import/export, plus writing to PNG, JPEG and WebP.
  • Performance claim: ArtCraft says VectorCraft can draw 20,000 shapes in approximately 27 milliseconds at Retina resolution — a shot across the bow of Electron-based design tools, and plausible given the Rust + GPU architecture proven in PhotoCraft.

Why this app matters most of the seven

Illustrator costs $22.99/month, has no browser version, no Linux version, and a file format that locks decades of design work into a subscription. The professional vector workflow is also more standardized than photo editing — pen, paths, booleans, export — which means a reimplementation doesn't need 35 years of features to be useful. A credible open-source vector editor with real SVG/PDF fidelity would be a bigger deal for working designers than PhotoCraft is for photographers.

The open question is AI/EPS file compatibility. The project's pattern is to lead with the incumbent's file format (PhotoCraft's PSD support is the proof), but as of October 2026 the published format list covers SVG and PDF — watch the release notes for native Illustrator file support before planning any migration.

VectorCraft vs Inkscape vs Affinity Designer

The free vector space already has incumbents, so where would VectorCraft fit? Inkscape is the most complete open-source vector editor, but its interface and color management feel their age, and Illustrator migrants regularly bounce off it. Affinity Designer (free since Canva's 2024 move) is the polished pro option — but it's closed-source, and "free" now lives inside Canva's ecosystem strategy, which makes some users nervous about the long term. VectorCraft's pitch is to be both things at once: Illustrator-familiar like Affinity, truly open like Inkscape, plus Rust-native performance neither matches. Whether it delivers depends on the two things we can't judge until an alpha ships: how complete the pen/path workflow feels in daily use, and whether Illustrator file compatibility ever arrives. Until then, Inkscape and Affinity remain the tools you can actually ship work with today.

Frequently asked questions

What is VectorCraft?

The vector-graphics editor in ArtCraft's Crafting Apps family — a free, open-source reimplementation of Adobe Illustrator written in Rust. Pen tool, booleans, gradient meshes, image tracing, SVG/PDF support. In development as of October 2026.

Is VectorCraft available to download?

It's in development. Builds and source appear via getartcraft.com/apps and github.com/storytold. Avoid unofficial mirrors.

How fast is VectorCraft?

ArtCraft claims 20,000 shapes rendered in approximately 27 milliseconds at Retina resolution, thanks to native Rust + GPU rendering.

What are the best free Illustrator alternatives right now?

Inkscape (open-source), Affinity Designer (free since 2024), and Figma's free tier for UI work — while VectorCraft matures.

Can VectorCraft open Illustrator AI files?

Not in the published feature list as of October 2026 — the announced formats are SVG and PDF import/export, plus PNG, JPEG and WebP output. Many AI files carry a PDF-compatible stream, so PDF import may partially work as a bridge, but native AI support is the feature to watch in the release notes.
