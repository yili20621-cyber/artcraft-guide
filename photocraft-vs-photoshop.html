PhotoCraft vs Photoshop: An Honest Comparison (2026)

One costs $0 and shipped its alpha weeks ago. The other costs $22.99/month and has 35 years of engineering behind it. Comparing them fairly means being honest about both. Here's the feature-by-feature breakdown — updated with the details that actually matter, like color management and file fidelity.

Where PhotoCraft genuinely wins

  • Price, and everything around it. No subscription, no annual-billing gotchas, no cancellation fees — the exact practices that cost Adobe a $150M federal settlement in March 2026. For anyone who opens a PSD a few times a month, Photoshop's ~$180+/year is hard to justify.
  • Print color on a budget. CMYK, Lab, 16/32-bit depth, ICC profiles and soft proofing in a free app is remarkable — GIMP users have waited decades for this. For checking print files without a subscription, PhotoCraft is now the best free answer.
  • Speed and weight. Native Rust with GPU rendering means near-instant launch and no background Creative Cloud services eating RAM.
  • Openness. Auditable source code, no telemetry, no account. You can verify what the software does with your files — with closed-source software you just have to trust.

Where Photoshop still dominates

  • The deep feature set. Smart objects, content-aware tools, Camera Raw, generative AI, advanced masking — PhotoCraft covers roughly a quarter to a third of the total. The missing portion is exactly what professionals use daily.
  • Reliability. Photoshop is battle-tested across 35 years of production pipelines. PhotoCraft is an alpha — a brilliant alpha, but an alpha.
  • Ecosystem. Plugins, actions, tutorials for every conceivable task, and every print shop and agency on earth expecting native PSD behavior.
  • Legal certainty. Adobe owns its IP outright. Some legal experts have flagged trade-dress risk in PhotoCraft's pixel-similar UI — unlikely to affect users, but it adds roadmap uncertainty.

So which should you use?

  • Use PhotoCraft if you're a hobbyist, student, or anyone whose Photoshop usage is "open PSD → basic edits → export" — or if you need free CMYK/ICC checking for print. It saves $180–276/year and honestly covers that workflow. Download guide →
  • Keep Photoshop if clients pay for your pixel work, your files lean on smart objects, or your pipeline depends on plugins and generative AI.
  • Use both — they're not mutually exclusive. Many people will keep PhotoCraft for quick jobs and print checks, Photoshop for the heavy ones, and re-evaluate each quarter as parity climbs.

What would change this comparison

Three milestones would move PhotoCraft from "great free option" to "genuine Photoshop threat": smart object support (the single most-requested pro feature), a plugin or scripting ecosystem (what keeps agencies on Adobe), and sustained stability — three to six months of real-world use without data-loss stories. Frequently asked questions

Is PhotoCraft as good as Photoshop?

No — not yet. PhotoCraft covers roughly a quarter to a third of Photoshop's feature set (October 2026). It wins on price, speed, print-color support for free, and openness; Photoshop wins on depth, stability, AI features and ecosystem.

How much does PhotoCraft cost compared to Photoshop?

PhotoCraft is free and open-source. Photoshop runs $14.99–22.99/month depending on plan — about $180–276 per year.

Can PhotoCraft open Photoshop PSD files?

Yes — layered PSD/PSB read and write, 134/135 round-trip tests passing, including CMYK and 16/32-bit documents. Smart-object-heavy files may lose information. Details in our PSD guide.

Which is better for print work?

Photoshop for professional production; PhotoCraft is the best free option for checking and lightly editing print files, thanks to CMYK/Lab support with ICC management and soft proofing.

Will PhotoCraft stay free?

The code is already public under open-source licenses, which can't be revoked for existing releases — so current versions stay free forever no matter what happens to the project.
