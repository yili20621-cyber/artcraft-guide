FilmCraft: ArtCraft's Open-Source Premiere Pro Alternative

In developmentFree & open-source

FilmCraft is the video editor in ArtCraft's Crafting Apps family — a from-scratch, Rust-written reimplementation of Adobe Premiere Pro. Its tagline: "Professional video editing, rebuilt from scratch." Of all seven apps, it's the most technically audacious — and the one with the longest road ahead.

Status as of October 2026

FilmCraft is in development, behind PhotoCraft (early alpha) in the family's release order. Builds and source are distributed through getartcraft.com/apps and github.com/storytold. Video editing is a much harder problem than raster editing — codecs, timeline rendering, color pipelines, audio sync — so expect FilmCraft's alpha to arrive later and rougher.

The feature set (as published by the project)

  • Editing core: source and program monitors, a multi-track timeline, 3-point editing and trimming — the professional NLE workflow, not a simplified consumer one.
  • Color: grading via curves, color wheels, HSL and LUTs, with HDR and Log video workflows.
  • Effects: approximately 55 video effects, 30 transitions, and keyframe editing.
  • Audio: an EBU R128 compliant loudness meter — a broadcast-standard detail that signals professional intent.
  • The audacious part — own codecs: FilmCraft doesn't use FFmpeg. It implements H.264, HEVC, ProRes, VP9, AAC and Opus itself, in Rust. Every other free editor (and many paid ones) wraps FFmpeg; writing codecs from scratch is a moonshot that either becomes a legendary engineering feat or the project's biggest maintenance burden.

Why this one is the hardest to pull off

Photo editing has one file format that matters (PSD). Video editing has dozens of acquisition formats, delivery formats, color spaces and hardware acceleration paths — plus Premiere's real moat: Dynamic Link with After Effects (which the family answers with the in-development EffectCraft), 15 years of third-party panels, and every post-production pipeline on earth. A credible FilmCraft alpha is exciting; a production-ready one is a much longer road. Plan accordingly.

The codec gamble, explained

Why does writing your own codecs matter? Every free video editor today — and honestly, many paid ones — handles media through FFmpeg, the 25-year-old open-source multimedia library. It's battle-tested, but it's also a shared dependency: licensing quirks (H.264/HEVC are patent-encumbered), platform quirks, and a codebase nobody fully controls. By implementing H.264, HEVC, ProRes, VP9, AAC and Opus natively in Rust, FilmCraft gets three things: memory safety in the most attack-prone part of a video app (decoding untrusted files is a classic exploit vector), consistent behavior across platforms, and freedom to optimize for its own timeline engine. The cost: years of edge-case hunting that FFmpeg already survived. When FilmCraft's alpha lands, the first thing to test isn't the effects — it's whether your camera's specific files decode cleanly. Bring your oddest footage: drone HEVC, old ProRes exports, screen recordings with weird frame rates. That's where a young codec stack shows its age.

Frequently asked questions

What is FilmCraft?

The video editor in ArtCraft's Crafting Apps family — a free, open-source reimplementation of Premiere Pro written in Rust. Multi-track timeline, 3-point editing, color grading with LUTs, ~55 effects, 30 transitions, EBU R128 loudness, and its own codecs. In development as of October 2026.

Is FilmCraft available to download?

It's in development. Builds and source appear via getartcraft.com/apps and github.com/storytold. Avoid unofficial mirrors.

Does FilmCraft use FFmpeg?

No — it implements H.264, HEVC, ProRes, VP9, AAC and Opus itself in Rust. One of the project's most ambitious technical choices.

What are the best free video editors right now?

DaVinci Resolve's free tier (pro-grade), CapCut (beginner-friendly), and Kdenlive (open-source) — while FilmCraft matures.

Can FilmCraft open Premiere project files?

Not that the project has announced as of October 2026. The family's pattern is to prioritize the incumbent's file formats — PhotoCraft leads with layered PSD — so Premiere project import is a reasonable thing to watch for, but don't plan a migration around it until it ships and is tested.
