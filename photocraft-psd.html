How to Open & Edit PSD Files Without Photoshop (Free)

Someone sent you a .psd and you don't have a $22.99/month Photoshop subscription. Good news: in 2026 you have real options — led by PhotoCraft, the free open-source editor whose layered PSD support is its headline feature. This guide covers exactly what survives the trip and what doesn't.

Option 1: PhotoCraft — best for real layered editing (and print files)

PhotoCraft reads and writes PSD and PSB files natively. Its compatibility suite passes 134 of 135 round-trip cases — open a PSD, save it, reopen it, and the structure survives. Unusually for a free editor, it also respects the file's color data: RGB, CMYK, Lab and grayscale at 8, 16 and 32-bit depth, with ICC color management and soft proofing.

Option 2: Photopea — best zero-install backup

Photopea is a free browser-based editor with excellent PSD compatibility. Nothing to install, works on any machine with a browser. Ads on the free tier; files are processed locally. It's the ideal second opinion when PhotoCraft — alpha software, after all — chokes on an unusual file.

Option 3: GIMP — the veteran, with caveats

GIMP opens PSDs and preserves basic layers, but compatibility is flatter: some blend modes, text layers and adjustment layers don't survive, and CMYK support remains a longstanding weak point. Fine for viewing and simple RGB edits; weaker for round-tripping files back to a Photoshop user, and not suitable for print files.

Step by step: open, edit, and export a PSD in PhotoCraft

  1. Duplicate the original file first. Work on the copy — non-negotiable with alpha software.
  2. Open the PSD. Check the layers panel against what you expect: groups, masks and adjustment layers should be intact. If the file opens flattened, stop — it likely contains smart objects, and editing here would bake them permanently.
  3. Check the color mode. If it's a print file, confirm the document is still CMYK (or Lab) at the original bit depth before touching anything. PhotoCraft preserves these, but verify — your print shop will not accept "the free app changed it" as an excuse.
  4. Make your edits using layers, masks and brushes as you would in Photoshop. Text edits require the same fonts installed locally.
  5. Save as PSD (keep layers) or export flattened PNG/JPEG/PDF for delivery.
  6. Round-trip test: reopen the saved file — in PhotoCraft, and ideally in Photoshop or Photopea — and confirm layers and color survived before deleting the original.

Which should you pick?

  • Edit PSDs regularly, want a desktop app → PhotoCraft
  • CMYK or 16/32-bit print files → PhotoCraft (the only free option that treats color seriously)
  • One-off file, someone else's computer → Photopea
  • Already a GIMP user, simple RGB file → GIMP
  • File full of smart objects → You still need Photoshop (or ask the sender to flatten/export)
Golden rule for round-trips: before editing an important PSD in any non-Adobe tool, duplicate the file. Edit the copy, save it, and re-open it to verify layers and color survived — especially with alpha-stage software like PhotoCraft.

Frequently asked questions

How can I open a PSD file without Photoshop?

Use PhotoCraft (free, open-source, layered PSD read/write), Photopea (free, browser-based), or GIMP (free, flatter compatibility). PhotoCraft and Photopea preserve layers best.

Does PhotoCraft preserve layers when opening PSD files?

Yes — layer stacks, masks and most blend modes survive; 134/135 round-trip tests pass, including CMYK and 16/32-bit files. Smart objects may flatten.

Can I save a PSD from PhotoCraft back to Photoshop?

Yes. PhotoCraft writes PSDs that reopen in Photoshop. Verify the round-trip on a copy of important files first.

Can I open a CMYK PSD without Photoshop?

Yes — PhotoCraft handles CMYK, Lab and grayscale at 8/16/32-bit with ICC management and soft proofing, so print files keep their color data.

Is there a free way to convert PSD to PNG/JPG?

All three tools above export flattened PNG/JPG. PhotoCraft's text/vector layers rasterize on export; Photopea is fastest for a one-off conversion in the browser.
