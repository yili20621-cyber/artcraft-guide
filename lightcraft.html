LightCraft: ArtCraft's Open-Source Lightroom Alternative

In developmentFree & open-source

LightCraft is the photo management and RAW development app in ArtCraft's Crafting Apps family — a from-scratch, Rust-written reimplementation of Adobe Lightroom. Its tagline: "Your photos. Your pixels. Your machine." Of all seven apps, this one may have the most pent-up demand.

Status as of October 2026

LightCraft is in development, behind PhotoCraft (early alpha) in the family's release order. Builds and source are distributed through getartcraft.com/apps and github.com/storytold. The demand context: Adobe's Photography plan crept from $9.99 to $14.99/month, and the March 2026 $150M settlement over cancellation practices pushed a lot of photographers to look for the exit — a free, local, open-source Lightroom is exactly what they're searching for.

The feature set (as published by the project)

  • Non-destructive editing: adjustments never alter the original file — the defining Lightroom behavior, implemented from day one.
  • Core develop tools: exposure, color, tone curves, an 8-band color mixer, and 3-way color grading.
  • Local adjustments: brushes and gradients, plus masks targeting brightness, color range, sky, subject and background — the AI-assisted masking workflow that modern Lightroom users consider essential.
  • Own RAW decoder: LightCraft processes DNG, CR2 (Canon), ARW (Sony), NEF (Nikon), RAF (Fujifilm), RW2 (Panasonic), PEF (Pentax) and ORF (Olympus) through its own Rust-written decoder — not a wrapper around LibRaw or dcraw. Like FilmCraft's own codecs, it's an ambitious choice that trades short-term speed for long-term control.

The honest caveat: it's all about the catalog

Lightroom's real moat isn't raw processing — darktable and RawTherapee already do that for free. The moat is the catalog: years of ratings, keywords, collections and edit histories that photographers can't afford to lose. LightCraft's published materials so far emphasize the develop module; the catalog and migration story is what to watch before planning any switch. And the golden rule applies doubly here: never point alpha software at your only photo archive.

LightCraft vs darktable: the comparison that matters

Photographers already have a free Lightroom alternative — darktable has been around for years. So why is LightCraft interesting? Three reasons. First, interface familiarity: darktable is powerful but famously idiosyncratic, while LightCraft deliberately mirrors Lightroom's layout, so migration costs are near zero. Second, modern masking: subject/sky/background masks with one click are what made recent Lightroom versions sticky, and LightCraft implements them from the start; darktable's parametric masks are capable but far more manual. Third, momentum: an open-source project moving at this speed attracts contributors, and contributors close feature gaps. The counterweight is maturity — darktable's camera support, noise handling and tethering are years deep. Our honest advice: keep darktable as your working tool, test LightCraft's alpha on a copied folder of a few hundred RAWs when it ships, and judge it on two things only — how your camera's files render, and whether the catalog story is real yet.

Frequently asked questions

What is LightCraft?

The photo management and RAW development app in ArtCraft's Crafting Apps family — a free, open-source reimplementation of Lightroom written in Rust. Non-destructive editing, 8-band color mixer, AI-assisted masks, own RAW decoder. In development as of October 2026.

Is LightCraft available to download?

It's in development. Builds and source appear via getartcraft.com/apps and github.com/storytold. Avoid unofficial mirrors.

Which RAW formats does LightCraft support?

DNG, CR2 (Canon), ARW (Sony), NEF (Nikon), RAF (Fujifilm), RW2 (Panasonic), PEF (Pentax) and ORF (Olympus), via its own Rust decoder.

What are the best free Lightroom alternatives right now?

darktable, RawTherapee and digiKam — all free and open-source — while LightCraft matures.

Can LightCraft import my existing Lightroom catalog?

No catalog-import feature has been announced as of October 2026 — and this is the single most important thing to watch. Years of ratings, keywords and edit histories live in that catalog. Until a tested migration path ships, keep Lightroom (or darktable) as the system of record and only experiment with copies of your files.
